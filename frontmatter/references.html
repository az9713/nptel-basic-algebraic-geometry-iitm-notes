Sources and references

The NPTEL recordings determine the course's topic spine. Standard mathematical references support explicit external inputs. The proofs and examples in this edition are independently written unless a passage names a research report.

Primary mathematical references used beside claims

  • The Stacks Project: Hilbert Nullstellensatz. Used for the weak theorem and the maximal-ideal dictionary.
  • The Stacks Project: Noether normalization and dimension of finite-type algebras. These are invoked algebraic inputs, not results inferred by counting equations.
  • The Stacks Project: Proj of a graded ring. The degree-zero localization construction provides a precise extension of the classical chart formulas.
  • The Stacks Project: regular local rings. Used to keep the local dimension criterion distinct from a Euclidean picture.
  • The Stacks Project: varieties and dominant rational maps. Used for the function-field interpretation of birationality.
  • Ravi Vakil, Introduction to Algebraic Geometry, especially Class 15, nonsingularity. Provides a primary teaching reference for the Jacobian and cotangent-space viewpoint. Individual chapters identify invoked results and their limits.

References are integrated beside the claims they support. This list is a navigation aid and does not imply that every theorem in the linked works is developed in this course.

Dated research extension

Lecture 21 records the historical Jacobian conjecture. Chapter 8 separately dates the November 2026 literature check and attributes the higher-dimensional counterexample report to Gao, Counterexamples to the Jacobian conjecture in dimensions greater than two. The plane-case status is discussed by Pissolato, On the Density of Polynomial Mappings Satisfying the Jacobian Conjecture. This edition does not claim independent verification of those research proofs.

Production precedents and software

The workflow and reading structure were calibrated against the Diffusion in Solids notes and the Stanford CS229 notes. Their prior reviews or tests do not validate this edition.

Mathematics is rendered with bundled MathJax 3.2.2, whose license is included. The reproducible algebra uses SymPy; parsing and validation use Beautiful Soup and Playwright. The pinned Python requirements and workflow accompany the original notes.

Raw captions, downloaded media, private source captures, and signed retrieval URLs are excluded from the repository. All source recordings and external works retain their own rights. This independent edition claims no NPTEL, IIT Madras, or Stanford endorsement.
