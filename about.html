Verification and limitations

This is an independent study edition covering the topic sequence of all forty-two supplied recordings. Its quality claims are separated into source evidence, mathematical review, executable checks, and reader validation.

What was used

The playlist and the official NPTEL course identify the recordings, order, lecturer, and scope. Official-label English caption tracks were acquired for every recording and retained privately. The public source manifest contains hashes and actual cue bounds. Caption text supports the topic spine and navigational locators; mathematical expressions are independently developed rather than trusted as transcriptions of the board.

Direct listening and video-frame inspection were not performed. The notes do not certify complete audiovisual coverage, every spoken aside, every board example, or uncaptioned tails. Selected caption spans and full sequential readings used by individual chapter authors are recorded in the production evidence; these different review depths are not collapsed into a single “fully verified source” label.

The exposition is original synthesis of the mathematical topics, with explicit external theorems and teaching constructions. It is not an official transcript and carries no NPTEL or university endorsement. A source title that omits a hypothesis is not used as an unrestricted mathematical assertion.

Mathematical and editorial review

Each chapter receives two independent science-editor reviews from agent contexts other than its author. Actual reports and content hashes are listed in the review index. A report's acceptance is an editorial judgment about the inspected fragment; it is not formal theorem-prover certification, a human referee report, or proof that every possible error has been eliminated.

Reviewers inspect definitions, assumptions, proof hinges, counterexamples, exercises and worked solutions. Repairs require targeted confirmation at the revised content hash. Major commutative-algebra inputs—such as the weak Nullstellensatz, normalization, the finite-type dimension theorem, and the principal ideal theorem—are identified as invoked results where they are not reproved.

Known hazards receive explicit treatment: Euclidean versus Zariski components of the real hyperbola; a bijective morphism versus an isomorphism; a point's local ring versus the function field; embedding-dependent homogeneous coordinate rings; and a singular locus versus a manifold boundary. The Jacobian conjecture has a separately dated research update, whose external proof is not independently reproduced here.

Reproducible and browser checks

The exact algebra report checks finite symbolic examples. The geometry report checks the plotted parametrizations. Neither establishes all the general theorems. The browser report records actual rendered math, errors, desktop and mobile layout, and controls. The package report checks inventory, links, anchors, math delimiters, and review hashes.

The bundled renderer permits local use without runtime network access. A screen-reader user may obtain different mathematical presentation support depending on the browser and assistive technology; that compatibility was not comprehensively tested. Print styles provide a reading layout, while browser print-to-PDF pagination can vary.

To reproduce the checks, install the pinned Python requirements, run the algebra and geometry scripts, rebuild the reader, and run the validators described in the repository README. Source captions are not needed to rebuild the authored reader; they are needed to repeat source acquisition and provenance extraction.

Publication boundary

The repository contains original notes, compact public provenance, reproducible code, reviewer reports, reader assets, and dependency licensing. It excludes captions, downloaded media, signed URLs, credentials, private working files, and personal biographical instructions. The course recordings and external references retain their own rights.

Corrections should identify the chapter, section, assertion, and a proof or counterexample. A mathematical correction takes precedence over matching an error in the spoken source. The source map retains the course's sequence while the notes preserve mathematical qualifications.
