Basic Algebraic Geometry

Varieties, morphisms, local rings, function fields, and nonsingularity. The organizing question is how a geometric property becomes a statement about functions—and which information is lost when we change rings.

These notes follow the 42-recording course by T. E. Venkata Balaji at IIT Madras, organized into fifteen chapters matching its topic modules. The exposition, examples, proofs, exercises, and code are independently written. The source map separates caption acquisition, topic coverage, and audiovisual limitations.

The mathematical route. Equations define closed sets; radical and prime ideals recover their geometry. Localization makes denominators legal on open sets. Local rings retain behavior near a point, while function fields retain only a dense open part. Tangent spaces then turn nonsingularity into a comparison between two dimensions.

How to use this edition

The assumed background is undergraduate abstract algebra and point-set topology. The notes refresh the ring-theoretic definitions needed for the arguments, but a first encounter with quotient rings, prime ideals, or field extensions may require the prerequisite route in the study guide.

Read a theorem's hypotheses before its proof. Work the running example by hand, then use the exact algebra laboratory to check the finite construction. Attempt an exercise before opening its solution. Recording timestamps are navigation aids, not claims that the surrounding exposition is a verbatim summary of that minute.

The reader works locally with its bundled MathJax renderer. Use the theme control, keyboard-accessible chapter navigation, and Print / PDF. There are no external fonts, analytics, or runtime content requests. See verification and limitations for the actual release evidence.
